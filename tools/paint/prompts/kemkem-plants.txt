A sheet of four separate plants for a natural-history museum diorama. Painted in the manner of Charles R. Knight (1874–1953), the American natural-history muralist: oil on canvas, confident visible brushwork, warm golden naturalistic light, soft atmospheric haze, a palette of ochres, siennas, olive greens and dusty blues, calm and dignified realism. Keep his warmth and his eye for the real world, but follow TODAY's science, not the science of his time: paint the land, water, plants and climate exactly as described here.
Each plant stands alone on a PURE WHITE background, with wide white space between them, in a row from left to right, all seen from about 45 degrees above (as from a low aircraft), lit from the upper left:
1) a low, rounded, leafy shrub about one and a half metres tall, with small leaves; 2) a clump of ferns about one metre tall; 3) a small low leafy plant from the water's edge, half a metre tall; 4) a more open, spreading low shrub.
They grow at south-east Morocco, about 100–95 million years ago: a few tall conifers related to monkey-puzzle trees (straight bare trunks, branches in tiers near the top) spaced along the far bank; low leafy shrubs and small plants at the water’s edge; a few ferns. Do not paint: grass or grassland, palm trees, cycads, broadleaf forest, flowers in bloom, cliffs, mountains, volcanoes.
No ground, no shadows, no soil, no pot, no text, numbers, labels, border or frame.
