A landscape painting for a natural-history museum: gouache and watercolour on warm paper, soft natural light, muted earthy colours, gentle visible brushwork, calm and documentary rather than dramatic or cinematic. A wide view at eye level, as if standing on a low sandbank.
Use the attached sketch for the layout ONLY: the far bank and horizon run straight across the picture at about 45% from the top; the river fills the band below it; a pale sandbank island sits right of centre in the river; a sandy near shore runs along the bottom edge. Keep the far bank low and level, and keep the sky open and plain, because small animal silhouettes will be placed on the bank, in the water and in the sky afterwards. Repaint everything: do not copy the sketch’s flat colours or shapes.
The place: south-east Morocco, about 100–95 million years ago.
Land: a low, level far bank of pale sand and reddish sandstone; a wide, flat near shore of sand and small pebbles.
Water: a broad, shallow, slow river, brownish-green, braided around a pale sandbank island.
Plants: a few tall conifers related to monkey-puzzle trees (straight bare trunks, branches in tiers near the top) spaced along the far bank; low leafy shrubs and small plants at the water’s edge; a few ferns.
Light and weather: a hot, hazy dry-season afternoon under a pale sky.
Do not paint: grass or grassland, palm trees, cycads, broadleaf forest, flowers in bloom, cliffs, mountains, volcanoes.
The picture must contain NO animals of any kind: no dinosaurs, reptiles, crocodiles, birds, pterosaurs, fish, insects or footprints; no people; no buildings, boats, roads or fences; no text, letters, signature, watermark, border or frame. Plants, land, water and sky only.
