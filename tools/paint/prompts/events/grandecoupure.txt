A painting for a natural-history museum: The Great Break, about 33.9 million years ago.
Painted in the manner of Charles R. Knight (1874–1953), the American natural-history muralist: oil on canvas, confident visible brushwork, warm golden naturalistic light, soft atmospheric haze, a palette of ochres, siennas, olive greens and dusty blues, calm and dignified realism. Keep his warmth and his eye for the real world, but follow TODAY's science, not the science of his time: paint the land, water, plants and climate exactly as described here.
What happened, from the scientific evidence: As Antarctica freezes, many of Europe’s mammals vanish and newcomers arrive from Asia.
Paint the moment as seen from high in the sky: a wide view of the land and sea where it happened, as they were THEN — the continents, coasts, plants and life of that time, not today's. Show only what the description says. Living things only where it names them, small, as they are reconstructed today; none where it does not.

A scientific reviewer turned down an earlier painting of this. Do NOT repeat any of these mistakes: Geographic compression of ice and temperate zones. Generic marine mammals. Modern geographical features like the Red Sea are present. The paleogeography is inaccurate for 33.9 million years ago. Animals are gigantically out of scale with the landmasses. Coastlines (Red Sea, India, Southeast Asia) are too modern for the Eocene-Oligocene boundary. The map shows modern geography, including the Red Sea and a solid Europe, plus modern-looking whales. The continental arrangement and animal species are highly anachronistic for 33.9 Ma. Modern continental arrangement including an open Red Sea and modern Mediterranean. Europe is shown as a solid landmass rather than an archipelago. Modern baleen whales instead of early mysticetes. Modern rhinos in Africa.
