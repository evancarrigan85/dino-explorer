A painting for a natural-history museum: The seas run short of oxygen, about 93.9 million years ago.
Painted in the manner of Charles R. Knight (1874–1953), the American natural-history muralist: oil on canvas, confident visible brushwork, warm golden naturalistic light, soft atmospheric haze, a palette of ochres, siennas, olive greens and dusty blues, calm and dignified realism. Keep his warmth and his eye for the real world, but follow TODAY's science, not the science of his time: paint the land, water, plants and climate exactly as described here.
What happened, from the scientific evidence: Oceanic Anoxic Event 2: huge eruptions warm the world, much of the ocean loses its oxygen, and some sea creatures die out.
Paint the moment as seen from high in the sky: a wide view of the land and sea where it happened, as they were THEN — the continents, coasts, plants and life of that time, not today's. Show only what the description says. Living things only where it names them, small, as they are reconstructed today; none where it does not.
No text, labels, map lines, arrows, borders or frame.
