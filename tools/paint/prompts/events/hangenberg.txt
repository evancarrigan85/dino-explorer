A painting for a natural-history museum: The Hangenberg crisis, about 359.3 million years ago.
Painted in the manner of Charles R. Knight (1874–1953), the American natural-history muralist: oil on canvas, confident visible brushwork, warm golden naturalistic light, soft atmospheric haze, a palette of ochres, siennas, olive greens and dusty blues, calm and dignified realism. Keep his warmth and his eye for the real world, but follow TODAY's science, not the science of his time: paint the land, water, plants and climate exactly as described here.
What happened, from the scientific evidence: At the very end of the Devonian, the armoured fishes and much other sea life die out, as ice spreads in the south and the seas lose oxygen.
Paint the moment as seen from high in the sky: a wide view of the land and sea where it happened, as they were THEN — the continents, coasts, plants and life of that time, not today's. Show only what the description says. Living things only where it names them, small, as they are reconstructed today; none where it does not.
What could not yet exist then, so must not be painted: NO palms, conifers, cycads or flowering plants. The trees are Archaeopteris: tall trees shaped like a conifer, with a straight trunk and spreading side branches carrying flat fern-like leaves — NOT palm-shaped — in forests along rivers and coasts, with low ferny and leafless forked plants and a litter of fallen branches beneath them. Plants grow as they do in nature: in continuous stands, thickets and carpets where there is water, thinning gradually where it is drier — never single plants dotted evenly over bare ground like a model.
A scientific reviewer turned down an earlier painting of this. Do NOT repeat any of these mistakes: Anachronistic palm trees are clearly visible on the landmasses. The presence of palm trees contradicts the scientific version. Modern palm trees are present instead of Archaeopteris. Some fish anatomy appears too modern or generic.
No text of any kind: no signature, monogram, initials or date (not even small, in a corner), no labels, map lines, arrows, border or frame.
