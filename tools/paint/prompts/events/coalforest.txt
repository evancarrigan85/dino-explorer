A painting for a natural-history museum: Coal-forest collapse, about 305 million years ago.
Painted in the manner of Charles R. Knight (1874–1953), the American natural-history muralist: oil on canvas, confident visible brushwork, warm golden naturalistic light, soft atmospheric haze, a palette of ochres, siennas, olive greens and dusty blues, calm and dignified realism. Keep his warmth and his eye for the real world, but follow TODAY's science, not the science of his time: paint the land, water, plants and climate exactly as described here.
What happened, from the scientific evidence: The tropical rainforests break up as the climate dries.
Paint the moment as seen from high in the sky: a wide view of the land and sea where it happened, as they were THEN — the continents, coasts, plants and life of that time, not today's. Show only what the description says. Living things only where it names them, small, as they are reconstructed today; none where it does not.
What could not yet exist then, so must not be painted: NO palms or flowering plants. The forests are giant club-moss trees (Lepidodendron, Sigillaria): tall straight bare trunks covered in a diamond-shaped scale pattern, ending in a few short forked branches with grassy tufts — NOT palm-shaped; with giant horsetails (Calamites, jointed stems with whorls of needle leaves) and tree ferns, standing close together in swamps over dark water, with fallen trunks and a dense undergrowth of ferns. Plants grow as they do in nature: in continuous stands, thickets and carpets where there is water, thinning gradually where it is drier — never single plants dotted evenly over bare ground like a model.
A scientific reviewer turned down an earlier painting of this. Do NOT repeat any of these mistakes: Palm-like trees are clearly visible in the foreground. Signature in the bottom right corner. Includes anachronistic plants and text. Anachronistic palm-like plants in foreground. Artist signature visible. Several trees in the foreground and midground have distinct palm-like crowns and trunks. Lycopsid trees lack proper dichotomous branching, contributing to their inaccurate palm-like appearance.
No text of any kind: no signature, monogram, initials or date (not even small, in a corner), no labels, map lines, arrows, border or frame.
