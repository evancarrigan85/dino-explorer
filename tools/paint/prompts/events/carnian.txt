A painting for a natural-history museum: The Carnian rains, about 233 million years ago.
Painted in the manner of Charles R. Knight (1874–1953), the American natural-history muralist: oil on canvas, confident visible brushwork, warm golden naturalistic light, soft atmospheric haze, a palette of ochres, siennas, olive greens and dusty blues, calm and dignified realism. Keep his warmth and his eye for the real world, but follow TODAY's science, not the science of his time: paint the land, water, plants and climate exactly as described here.
What happened, from the scientific evidence: One to two million years of wet climate. Many kinds of life die out — and dinosaurs spread straight afterwards.
Paint the moment as seen from high in the sky: a wide view of the land and sea where it happened, as they were THEN — the continents, coasts, plants and life of that time, not today's. Show only what the description says. Living things only where it names them, small, as they are reconstructed today; none where it does not.
What could not yet exist then, so must not be painted: NO palm trees (palms appear about 100 million years ago) and nothing palm-shaped (no tall slender trunk with a crown of big fan or feather leaves), and NO flowering plants or flowers. The trees are conifers (pine-like and monkey-puzzle-like), ginkgos, tree ferns, and cycads — cycads only as SHORT, stout, barrel-trunked plants no taller than a person. Where it is wet the ground is covered with ferns and horsetails (there is no grass), in thick stands; where it is dry, scattered low shrubs and bare ground. Plants grow as they do in nature: in continuous stands, thickets and carpets where there is water, thinning gradually where it is drier — never single plants dotted evenly over bare ground like a model.
A scientific reviewer turned down an earlier painting of this. Do NOT repeat any of these mistakes: Signature text present. Shows palms and text. Signature in bottom right. Numerous palm-shaped trees with tall slender trunks and frond crowns are prominent in the foreground. The inclusion of palm-like trees violates the botanical constraints for this time period. The crocodilian animals resemble modern crocodiles too closely; phytosaurs or early crocodylomorphs would be more accurate. Some trees on the right appear to have broad leaves, suggesting angiosperms, which did not exist yet. Several tall, slender plants with frond crowns resembling palms are prominent in the foreground. A signature is visible in the bottom right corner. It includes forbidden palm-shaped plants and text. Artist signature included in the bottom right corner. Foreground plants too closely resemble modern palms, violating Triassic flora constraints.
No text of any kind: no signature, monogram, initials or date (not even small, in a corner), no labels, map lines, arrows, border or frame.
