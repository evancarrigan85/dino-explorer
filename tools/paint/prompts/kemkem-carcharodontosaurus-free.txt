Paint Carcharodontosaurus alive, seen exactly from the side, the whole animal from the tip of the snout to the tip of the tail, filling most of the picture's width.
Painted in the manner of Charles R. Knight (1874–1953), the American natural-history muralist: oil on canvas, confident visible brushwork, warm golden naturalistic light, soft atmospheric haze, a palette of ochres, siennas, olive greens and dusty blues, calm and dignified realism. Keep his warmth and his eye for the real world, but follow TODAY's science, not the science of his time: paint the land, water, plants and climate exactly as described here.
How Carcharodontosaurus is reconstructed today: a giant two-legged meat-eater with a long, deep skull and blade-like serrated teeth, short arms with three fingers, powerful hind legs, and a long tail held level off the ground; scaly skin; its colours are not known, so muted earth tones.
Warm afternoon light from the upper left. A plain, flat, pure white background: no ground, no shadow, no scenery, no other animals, no text, labels, border or frame.
