How this map is made

The land moves the way the science says it did. Every piece of today's coastline is attached to the tectonic plate it sits on and carried back to where that plate was — using a published reconstruction of plate motions covering the last billion years (Merdith and colleagues, 2021).

The outlines are today's coasts. That makes continents recognisable, but real shorelines changed as seas rose and fell — for instance, a seaway covered the middle of North America in the Cretaceous. Before 410 million years ago the map shows the model's ancient continental blocks instead.

Green, tan and white are a sketch, not a measurement: a simple picture of wet, dry and cold belts by latitude, with ice where the rocks record ice ages. Before plants reached land, the land is drawn bare.

Fossil markers sit where each fossil was found, moved to where that spot was when the animal was alive.
